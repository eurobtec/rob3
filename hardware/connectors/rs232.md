By using a CMOS inverter instead of a dedicated MAX232 chip, the designer created a highly cost-effective, passive RS232-to-TTL receiver stage.

## Signal Directions & Operation
- **Top Line (Receive):** The PC sends data out from DB9 Pin 2. It drops across the 10k/100k safety network, enters input Pin 13 of the MM74C04N CMOS inverter, is flipped to safe 5V TTL logic, and leaves Pin 12 to enter the 8031 RXD Pin 10.
- **Bottom Line (Transmit):** The 8031 TXD Pin 11 sends out 0–5V serial data. It passes through a 100k current-limiting resistor into the inverting input Pin 2 of the M34004 Op-Amp. Powered by ±9V, the op-amp boosts and flips this signal out of Pin 1 straight to DB9 Pin 3.

> **Note (DB9 pin 5 = Signal Ground, buffered by M34004 OUT4).** DB9 pin 5 is
> the serial **Signal Ground (≈ 0 V)** — it mates with a normal PC's pin-5 GND,
> so it must be at 0 V. On this board it is **not** a bare tie to system GND:
> it is fed by **M34004 pin 14 (OUTPUT 4)**, whose channel buffers the op-amp
> array's **0 V reference baseline** (pins 3/12 reference line) and presents it
> as Signal Ground to the host. INPUT 4− (pin 13) is driven from 8031 P3.5/T1,
> which the firmware never toggles (TMOD=0x21 uses T1 as the baud generator
> only), so the input is static — consistent with a reference buffer, not a data
> driver. The exact pin-5 voltage is **[INFER] ≈ 0 V until metered** (it has to
> be ~0 V to connect to a PC ground). See `../board/M34004.md`.
